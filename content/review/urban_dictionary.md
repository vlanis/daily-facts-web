- [~] **1.** **Ghosting** — cutting off all contact with someone with no explanation and no warning, usually in dating. *"He ghosted me after three dates."* The term went mainstream around 2015 and is now used in hiring too, where candidates and employers ghost each other alike.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage; Merriam-Webster «ghosting»*

- [~] **2.** **To flex** — to show off, usually about money, looks or success. *"He's just flexing."* Comes from flexing a muscle. The reply *"weird flex but OK"* means: that is an odd thing to brag about.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **3.** **Salty** — bitter or annoyed, especially about losing. *"Don't be salty just because you lost."* Originally US Navy slang for a bad-tempered old sailor, revived by gamers in the 2010s.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **4.** **To spill the tea** — to share gossip. *"Come on, spill the tea."* Here *tea* means the gossip itself, thought to come from *T* for "truth". Related: *the tea is piping hot* — the gossip is very good.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **5.** **Low-key / high-key** — *low-key* means slightly or secretly: *"I'm low-key nervous."* *High-key* is its opposite, meaning openly and very much: *"I'm high-key obsessed with this song."* Both work as adverbs in a way no dictionary quite sanctions.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **6.** **To hit different** — to feel unusually good or strike you unusually hard. *"This song hits different at 3am."* Grammatically it should be *differently*; the missing -ly is the whole point of the phrase.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **7.** **Sus** — short for suspicious or suspect. *"That's kind of sus."* The word existed as British police shorthand for decades, then exploded worldwide in 2020 through the game Among Us.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage; British «sus laws» terminology*

- [~] **8.** **To touch grass** — an instruction to go outside and reconnect with reality, aimed at someone who is too invested in something online. *"Mate, touch grass."* Rude, but rarely meant as a serious insult.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **9.** **The ick** — a sudden, irreversible feeling of revulsion toward someone you were attracted to, usually triggered by something trivial. *"He ran for the bus and I got the ick."* The defining feature is that it cannot be reasoned away.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **10.** **Rent free** — occupying your thoughts without you wanting it to. *"That comment lives rent free in my head."* Always used about something you would rather stop thinking about.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **11.** **Cap / no cap** — *cap* means a lie, so *no cap* means "I'm serious, no exaggeration". *"That was the best meal I've had all year, no cap."* From older Black American slang where *capping* meant boasting.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **12.** **Mid** — disappointingly average. *"The film was mid."* Harsher than it sounds: calling something *mid* dismisses it more thoroughly than calling it bad, because bad is at least interesting.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **13.** **To gatekeep** — to guard access to a hobby or interest by quizzing newcomers on whether they are real fans. *"Stop gatekeeping, she's allowed to like the band."*
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **14.** **Main character energy** — behaving as though the world is your story. Can be a compliment (confidence) or a jab (self-absorption), and the tone decides which. *"She walked in with full main character energy."*
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **15.** **To be down bad** — to be embarrassingly desperate, usually romantically. *"He's texting her at 2am, he's down bad."* Almost always said about someone else, rarely admitted about oneself.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **16.** **Rizz** — charm, the ability to attract someone by talking to them. Shortened from *charisma*. Oxford named it word of the year in 2023, which many users felt instantly killed it.
  <br>↳ *Oxford Word of the Year 2023*

- [~] **17.** **To slap** — to be excellent, said of music or food. *"This song slaps."* One of the rare slang verbs that works with no object at all.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **18.** **Delulu** — a playful shortening of *delusional*, used about your own unrealistic hopes rather than as an insult. *"I'm delulu but I think he likes me."* Often paired with the mock-motivational "delulu is the solulu".
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **19.** **Doomscrolling** — reading distressing news for far longer than you meant to, unable to stop. *"I doomscrolled until 3am."* The word spread in 2020 and made several dictionaries' words-of-the-year lists.
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster; dictionary word-of-the-year lists (2020)*

- [~] **20.** **To leave someone on read** — to see a message and not reply, when the sender can see you have read it. *"She left me on read."* A form of rejection that only exists because of read receipts.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **21.** **Bussin'** — very good, said almost exclusively about food. *"This is bussin'."* From African American Vernacular English, and a common example of slang that spread far beyond its origin without credit.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage; AAVE origins*

- [~] **22.** **Side quest** — an unplanned detour from what you were supposed to be doing, borrowed from video games. *"I went out for milk and ended up on a three-hour side quest."*
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **23.** **To be cooked** — to be in serious trouble or beyond saving. *"I haven't revised at all, I'm cooked."* The opposite, *let him cook*, means leave someone alone because their odd plan is working.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **24.** **Girl dinner** — an assembled non-meal of snacks eaten instead of cooking: cheese, olives, crisps, whatever is in the fridge. *"Girl dinner tonight."* Named in 2023, though the practice is obviously much older.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage (2023)*

- [~] **25.** **NPC** — someone behaving as though running on a script, with no original thought, from *non-player character* in games. *"He gives NPC energy."* Fairly cutting, so used carefully.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [~] **26.** **To understand the assignment** — to have grasped exactly what was needed and delivered it. *"That outfit understood the assignment."* Praise, and applied to things as often as to people.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [ ] **27.** **Situationship** — a romantic relationship that is more than friendship but has never been defined or committed to. *"Are you two dating?" — "It's more of a situationship."* Oxford shortlisted it for Word of the Year 2023.
  <br>↳ *Oxford Languages, 2023*

- [ ] **28.** **To gaslight** — to manipulate someone into doubting their own memory or judgement. *"Stop gaslighting me, I know what you said."* From the 1944 film «Gaslight»; Merriam-Webster's Word of the Year 2022.
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster, 2022*

- [ ] **29.** **Brain rot** — the supposed mental decline from scrolling trivial online content, and the content itself. *"Two hours of short videos — pure brain rot."* Oxford's Word of the Year 2024.
  <br>↳ *Oxford Languages, 2024*

- [ ] **30.** **Goated** — being the greatest, from *GOAT* (Greatest Of All Time). *"Her cooking is goated."*
  <br>↳ *Urban Dictionary; Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **31.** **Lit** — exciting, excellent, full of energy, usually about a party or event. *"That concert was lit."* Older slang for drunk, which still survives in some contexts.
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **32.** **Vibe check** — a quick assessment of someone's mood or a place's atmosphere. *"Vibe check: how's everyone feeling?"* "Failing the vibe check" means giving off a bad impression.
  <br>↳ *Dictionary.com*

- [ ] **33.** **FOMO** — fear of missing out, the anxiety that others are having fun without you. Its opposite, **JOMO**, is the joy of missing out — happily staying in.
  <br>↳ *Oxford English Dictionary*

- [ ] **34.** **Hangry** — so hungry that you become irritable. *"Don't talk to me before lunch, I'm hangry."* Added to the Oxford English Dictionary in 2018.
  <br>↳ *Oxford English Dictionary, 2018*

- [ ] **35.** **To ratio** — on social media, to get more replies or likes on a reply than the original post got, usually signalling that people disagree with the original. *"He got ratioed."*
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **36.** **Stan** — an extremely devoted fan; also a verb. *"I stan this band."* From Eminem's 2000 song «Stan» about an obsessive fan. Added to the OED in 2019.
  <br>↳ *Oxford English Dictionary, 2019*

- [ ] **37.** **Simp** — someone who does far too much for a person they're attracted to, hoping to win them over. Mocking, often unfair. *"He drove four hours just to bring her coffee — total simp."*
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **38.** **Bet** — "okay, deal, I agree". *"See you at eight?" — "Bet."* A short, confident yes.
  <br>↳ *Urban Dictionary; Dictionary.com*

- [ ] **39.** **Slay** — to do something brilliantly, especially look great or perform. *"You slayed that presentation."*
  <br>↳ *Dictionary.com*

- [ ] **40.** **It's giving…** — "it has the vibe of…". *"That outfit — it's giving 1970s."* Often cut short: *"It's giving."* simply means "this is great".
  <br>↳ *Dictionary.com*

- [ ] **41.** **Periodt** — an emphatic "period", ending an argument: what I said is final. *"She's the best singer alive, periodt."*
  <br>↳ *Dictionary.com; African-American Vernacular English*

- [ ] **42.** **Basic** — unoriginal, mainstream in an uninteresting way. *"Pumpkin spice latte and a fall selfie? So basic."*
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **43.** **Extra** — over the top, dramatic, doing far more than needed. *"She brought a cake, balloons and a speech — so extra."*
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **44.** **Shook** — shocked or shaken. *"I'm still shook from that ending."*
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **45.** **Snatched** — looking great, particularly in fit or style. *"Your outfit is snatched."*
  <br>↳ *Dictionary.com*

- [ ] **46.** **To throw shade** — to insult someone subtly or indirectly. *"Was that comment shade?"* From New York drag ball culture, documented in the 1990 film «Paris Is Burning».
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **47.** **Yeet** — to throw something with force; also an exclamation of excitement. *"He yeeted his phone across the room."* Spread by a 2014 Vine dance video.
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **48.** **Boujee** — acting fancy or high-class, sometimes pretentiously. From French *bourgeois*. *"Dinner at that place is too boujee for me."*
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **49.** **Clapback** — a sharp, witty response to criticism. *"Her clapback to the troll went viral."*
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **50.** **Glow-up** — a dramatic improvement in appearance or life, usually over time. *"Have you seen her glow-up since school?"*
  <br>↳ *Merriam-Webster*

- [ ] **51.** **To ship** — to want two people (real or fictional) to be in a relationship. From *relationship*. *"I totally ship them."*
  <br>↳ *Oxford English Dictionary*

- [ ] **52.** **Fam** — close friends, as if family. *"What's up, fam?"* Common in American and British street slang for decades, now used everywhere online.
  <br>↳ *Oxford English Dictionary*
