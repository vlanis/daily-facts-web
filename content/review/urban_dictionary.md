- [x] **1.** **Ghosting** — cutting off all contact with someone with no explanation and no warning, usually in dating. *"He ghosted me after three dates."* The term went mainstream around 2015 and is now used in hiring too, where candidates and employers ghost each other alike.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage; Merriam-Webster «ghosting»*

- [x] **2.** **To flex** — to show off, usually about money, looks or success. *"He's just flexing."* Comes from flexing a muscle. The reply *"weird flex but OK"* means: that is an odd thing to brag about.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [x] **3.** **Salty** — bitter or annoyed, especially about losing. *"Don't be salty just because you lost."* Originally US Navy slang for a bad-tempered old sailor, revived by gamers in the 2010s.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [x] **4.** **To spill the tea** — to share gossip. *"Come on, spill the tea."* Here *tea* means the gossip itself, thought to come from *T* for "truth". Related: *the tea is piping hot* — the gossip is very good.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [x] **5.** **Low-key / high-key** — *low-key* means slightly or secretly: *"I'm low-key nervous."* *High-key* is its opposite, meaning openly and very much: *"I'm high-key obsessed with this song."* Both work as adverbs in a way no dictionary quite sanctions.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [x] **6.** **To hit different** — to feel unusually good or strike you unusually hard. *"This song hits different at 3am."* Grammatically it should be *differently*; the missing -ly is the whole point of the phrase.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [x] **7.** **Sus** — short for suspicious or suspect. *"That's kind of sus."* The word existed as British police shorthand for decades, then exploded worldwide in 2020 through the game Among Us.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage; British «sus laws» terminology*

- [x] **8.** **To touch grass** — an instruction to go outside and reconnect with reality, aimed at someone who is too invested in something online. *"Mate, touch grass."* Rude, but rarely meant as a serious insult.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [x] **9.** **The ick** — a sudden, irreversible feeling of revulsion toward someone you were attracted to, usually triggered by something trivial. *"He ran for the bus and I got the ick."* The defining feature is that it cannot be reasoned away.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*

- [x] **10.** **Rent free** — occupying your thoughts without you wanting it to. *"That comment lives rent free in my head."* Always used about something you would rather stop thinking about.
  <br>↳ *English slang usage*
